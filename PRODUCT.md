# Product

## Register

brand

## Name

**ARABART**, after Yasser Awad's Arabic graphics library, and because his code is art. The repo and the URL path are both `ARABART`.

## Users

- Family, friends and former colleagues of Yasser Awad, who come to see his work and share it.
- Modern developers in 2026 who are good at their job but have never touched DOS. If a sentence needs DOS knowledge to understand, it fails. Explain with what they already know (the browser, JavaScript, databases, Git, phones).
- Retro-computing and Arabic-computing fans, who come to run the programs and type with ARABART.
- Assume ADHD for everyone: the most exciting fact first, short sentences, depth is opt-in.
- Most arrive from a shared link on a phone or laptop. They should be able to run a program or type Arabic in ARABART in one tap.

## Product Purpose

A proud archive of Yasser Awad's work: the 15 DOS programs he wrote in Port Said, Egypt, from 1991 to 1993, his ARABART library, his career (Sakhr; Newbridge Networks → Alcatel, Kanata; DinarSoft and its award-winning Windows Mobile apps), and his patents. Every program runs in the browser. Every idea is shown with a live, animated, interactive explanation. The flagship is ARABART itself: a real JavaScript port of his library that lets anyone type Arabic and watch his 1993 engine join the letters live. Success: a visitor leaves impressed by an Egyptian engineer who wrote beautiful code with no internet, and wants to share it.

## Brand Personality

Proud, warm, human. This is a family's tribute and it takes Yasser's side: it celebrates what he built and never lists bugs, weak spots or limits. It never hedges or doubts him ("likely", "claims", "according to his CV", "no public source"); state the family's account as fact, and quote him where we can. Language is plain and flowing, like a friend explaining something they love; no jargon without an everyday comparison. The identity is "Joined": the Borland Turbo C IDE he coded in (editor blue, yellow text, cyan, gray menu bar, double-line windows), plus kinetic Arabic letters that change shape as they join. DinarSoft pages carry DinarSoft's own logo and look.

## Anti-references

- Generic "retro terminal" templates: page-wide scanlines over body text, glow on every element, uniform card grids.
- SaaS landing-page grammar: hero metrics, eyebrow labels, identical feature cards.
- AI-analysis voice: identical section templates, TL;DR blocks on every page, question headings everywhere, "we measured", grading or psychologizing him.
- Novelty that costs clarity: hidden navigation, keyboard-only flows, text the visitor has to decode.

## Design Principles

1. Show, then tell. Every explanation is a live, animated thing you can touch: type, drag, scrub, step. Text supports it.
2. The work is the hero. His running programs, his letters and his screens carry the visuals; effects frame them.
3. Plain words. A modern developer understands every sentence on first read.
4. Proud and true. Every fact comes from his binaries, the archives or the family; nothing is invented, nothing is hedged.
5. Fast everywhere. Smooth on a mid-range phone; shaders and heavy motion scale down on weak devices and turn off for reduced motion.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA: text contrast ≥ 4.5:1, visible focus, 44px touch targets, semantic landmarks.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no boot sequence, no flicker; instant state changes; every interactive explanation still works.
- Arabic text is marked with `lang="ar"` and `dir="rtl"`.
- Emulators need a keyboard; the page offers on-screen keys for every program that needs typing.
